# Regenerative Resource Cycle

Reduce extraction and leave the system with more usable capacity. Never combine unlike resources into a single unsupported net-positive score.

## Systemic contract

A valid proposal benefits at least three of the human, social, knowledge, resources, and ecology lenses through named mechanisms and indicators. It records impacts in all five, pairs a reinforcing circularity loop with a safeguard against rebound or burden shifting, leaves a repairable asset or restored resource, compares each resource type with a baseline, and labels expected gains as hypotheses until measured.

Before action, name at least one resource or capability expected to end above baseline and set a limit for every material resource. After action, call the outcome regenerative only when indicators support that threshold; a gain in one unit does not erase a deficit in another.

## Workflow

1. Define the useful function, users, place, time horizon, system boundary, and baseline. Do not assume the current product or process must survive.
2. Map the lifecycle: sourcing, production, transport, use, maintenance, data processing, recovery, and end of life. Include time and attention, not only materials and money.
3. Keep separate ledgers for material, energy, water, money, compute, data, time, and attention. Record units, source, owner, uncertainty, and externalized effects.
4. Apply the hierarchy in order: avoid, reduce, share, maintain, repair, reuse, refurbish, remanufacture, recycle, then safely dispose.
5. Check who gains, who performs unpaid or hazardous work, which knowledge is transferred, and which ecological threshold could be crossed.
6. Design a return path, steward, maintenance plan, accessible instructions, and incentive that makes the next cycle easier.
7. Check rebound: lower cost or friction may increase total use. Add a cap, trigger, quota, budget, or review rule where needed.
8. Pilot the smallest reversible change. Measure each resource ledger and at least three systemic indicators before deciding to adopt, adapt, or stop.

## Output

Start with a plain-language recommendation. Then provide the lifecycle boundary, separate resource ledgers, loss points, hierarchy options, chosen loop, rebound safeguard, five-lens impacts, restoration commitment, owners, indicators, pilot, stop condition, and review date.

Respond in the user's language. Define specialized terms on first use and preserve precise terms, evidence, uncertainty, and caveats in the technical layer.

## Boundaries

Do not claim circularity from recycling alone, count avoided harm as restoration, offset one resource with an unrelated resource, or export waste and risk to another community. Use domain experts for hazardous materials, environmental compliance, or lifecycle assessment.
