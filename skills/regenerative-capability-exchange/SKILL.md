# Regenerative Capability Exchange

Start from strengths already present. The goal is not merely to transfer information, but to make useful capability more distributed and renewable.

## Systemic contract

A valid exchange shows mechanisms and indicators in at least three of the human, social, knowledge, resources, and ecology lenses; checks the others for harm; pairs a teach-and-reteach loop with safeguards against exploitation and gatekeeping; leaves reusable learning and stronger relationships; budgets preparation, access, compensation, tools, and follow-up; and treats expected independence as a testable hypothesis.

Before action, name at least one resource or capability expected to end above baseline and set a limit for every material resource. After action, call the outcome regenerative only when indicators support that threshold; a gain in one unit does not erase a deficit in another.

## Workflow

1. Define the capability people want to gain, the real task it enables, and how learners will know they can act without the facilitator.
2. Map existing gifts, lived experience, tools, spaces, relationships, and community knowledge alongside gaps. Do not describe people only by needs.
3. Establish voluntary participation, accessibility, consent, attribution, fair compensation or reciprocity, and boundaries for culturally restricted or sensitive knowledge.
4. Pair small complementary exchanges. Use demonstration, supported practice, reflection, and teach-back on a real task.
5. Rotate roles so learners teach, document, adapt, and facilitate. Create more than one holder for every critical capability.
6. Produce a plain-language guide with precise terms, examples, failure modes, and a path to deeper technical material.
7. Measure successful independent use, peer-to-peer transfer, network diversity, confidence calibrated against performance, burden, and access gaps.
8. Return results to participants and let them choose whether to continue, redesign, compensate differently, or stop.

## Output

Create a capability-exchange plan containing purpose, existing assets, participants and access needs, reciprocity agreement, learning sequence, teach-back task, role rotation, reusable asset, resource budget, five-lens indicators, safeguard, owner, and review date.

Respond in the user's language. Define specialized terms on first use and preserve precise terms, evidence, uncertainty, and caveats in the technical layer.

## Boundaries

Do not extract unpaid expertise, treat community knowledge as ownerless, manufacture participation, or use confidence alone as proof of competence. Credentials, clinical practice, safety-critical work, and regulated professions require qualified supervision.
