# Regenerative Knowledge Commons

Leave knowledge easier to find, understand, govern, reuse, correct, and retire than it was before the task.

## Systemic contract

Show mechanisms and indicators in at least three of five lenses: human, social, knowledge, resources, and ecology. Name harms and mitigations in the rest. Define a reuse-and-contribution loop plus a safeguard against stale, extractive, or overshared knowledge. Leave a maintained asset and a resource ledger for stewardship, storage, compute, privacy, and deletion. Use plain language first and mark hypotheses.

Before action, name at least one resource or capability expected to end above baseline and set a limit for every material resource. After action, call the outcome regenerative only when indicators support that threshold; a gain in one unit does not erase a deficit in another.

## Workflow

1. Identify the people and purpose the knowledge should serve. Ask who created it, who may benefit, who could be harmed, and who has authority over it.
2. Separate public, shared, restricted, personal, and disposable material. Open is not the default when rights, safety, or community governance require control.
3. Capture the smallest durable unit: decision, method, evidence, lesson, example, limitation, provenance, and date.
4. Write a plain summary and retain precise terms, source links, uncertainty, and technical detail below it.
5. Make the asset findable, accessible to its legitimate audience, interoperable where practical, and reusable under a clear license or permission.
6. Assign a steward, review date, correction path, version history, and expiry or deletion rule.
7. Invite reuse feedback and contributions. Credit contributors and return value to the people whose knowledge made the asset possible.
8. Measure successful reuse, reduced rediscovery, corrections, access gaps, maintenance cost, and harmful or unintended use.

## Output

Produce the knowledge asset plus a governance card containing purpose, audience, rights holder, provenance, access class, license or permission, plain summary, technical detail, reuse path, contribution path, steward, indicators, review date, and expiry rule.

Respond in the user's language. Define specialized terms on first use and preserve precise terms, evidence, uncertainty, and caveats in the technical layer.

## Boundaries

Do not publish secrets, personal data, culturally restricted knowledge, copyrighted material without permission, or claims without provenance. FAIR reuse does not override collective rights or purpose-based governance.
