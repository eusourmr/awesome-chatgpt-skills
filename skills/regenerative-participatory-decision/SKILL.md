# Regenerative Participatory Decision

Help affected people shape a real decision. Participation is invalid when the outcome is already fixed or feedback cannot change anything.

## Systemic contract

Require concrete benefits and indicators in at least three of five lenses: human, social, knowledge, resources, and ecology. Record spillovers, a reinforcing trust-and-learning loop, a balancing rule against capture or endless meetings, a reusable decision record, and the attention, time, money, data, and access budget. Treat expected benefits as hypotheses until reviewed.

Before action, name at least one resource or capability expected to end above baseline and set a limit for every material resource. After action, call the outcome regenerative only when indicators support that threshold; a gain in one unit does not erase a deficit in another.

## Workflow

1. Define the decision, who has authority, what is negotiable, the deadline, and the consequences of doing nothing.
2. Map people who decide, implement, use, are indirectly affected, or are usually absent. Note power, access, language, disability, time, and safety barriers.
3. Choose the lightest legitimate mode: inform, consult, collaborate, consent, or delegate. Do not use consensus for cheap, reversible decisions.
4. Give participants the same plain-language brief, technical appendix, options, constraints, and conflict-of-interest disclosure.
5. Collect proposals and dissent without forcing premature agreement. Protect minority positions from being averaged away.
6. Compare options across the five lenses, including delayed and externalized costs.
7. Apply the declared decision rule. Record the decision, reasoning, dissent, commitments, owners, and uncertainties.
8. Return to every participant with what was accepted, rejected, or deferred and why.
9. Review outcomes on a named date. Reopen the decision if a stop trigger, excluded harm, or false assumption appears.

## Output

Produce a plain summary followed by a decision record containing scope, authority, participation mode, affected and missing groups, options, five-lens impacts, decision rule, dissent, commitments, feedback message, resource budget, review date, and reopening triggers.

Respond in the user's language. Define specialized terms on first use and preserve precise terms, evidence, uncertainty, and caveats in the technical layer.

## Boundaries

Never contact people, run a vote, publish feedback, or make the decision without authorization. Do not expose personal or sensitive input. Do not claim representation when the sample excludes affected groups.
