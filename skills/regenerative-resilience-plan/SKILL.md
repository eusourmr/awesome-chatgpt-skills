# Regenerative Resilience Plan

Protect essential functions while increasing shared capacity for the next disruption. Resilience is not asking vulnerable people to tolerate preventable harm.

## Systemic contract

Require benefits and indicators in at least three of the human, social, knowledge, resources, and ecology lenses; account for all five; combine a preparedness-and-learning loop with safeguards against burnout, centralization, and maladaptation; leave reusable capacity; budget response and recovery resources separately; and distinguish tested capability from planned capability.

Before action, name at least one resource or capability expected to end above baseline and set a limit for every material resource. After action, call the outcome regenerative only when indicators support that threshold; a gain in one unit does not erase a deficit in another.

## Workflow

1. Define the essential functions, minimum acceptable service, affected population, place, time horizon, and decision authority.
2. Map hazards, exposure, vulnerability, existing capacity, critical dependencies, single points of failure, and cascading effects. Ask who is hit first and recovers last.
3. Create scenarios that include slow stress, sudden shock, compound events, and failure of a trusted dependency. State assumptions and uncertainty.
4. Design measures for prevention, absorption, adaptation, recovery, and transformation. Prefer diversity, redundancy, modularity, local agency, and graceful degradation where proportionate.
5. Assign decision rights, accessible communications, backups, mutual-aid relationships, resource reserves, and escalation routes. Avoid making one person the backup for the whole system.
6. Define leading indicators, harm indicators, activation thresholds, stop conditions, recovery objectives, and restoration commitments.
7. Run a tabletop exercise or small reversible drill with informed participants. Record surprises, missing voices, bottlenecks, and unsafe workload.
8. Update the plan, return the learning to participants, and schedule the next test. Track whether capability becomes more distributed over time.

## Output

Give a plain-language readiness summary followed by essential functions, risk and dependency map, affected and missing groups, scenario assumptions, prevention-to-transformation measures, roles, accessible communication paths, resource reserves, indicators, triggers, drill, recovery criteria, restoration plan, and review date.

Respond in the user's language. Define specialized terms on first use and preserve precise terms, evidence, uncertainty, and caveats in the technical layer.

## Boundaries

Do not invent emergency contacts, guarantees, legal requirements, or hazard probabilities. Do not expose sensitive infrastructure details. For imminent danger, direct people to appropriate emergency services and qualified local authorities.
